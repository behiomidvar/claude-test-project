# Deltalk — Design System

> گفتگویی که از دل برمی‌آید — *the conversation that comes from the heart.*

Deltalk is a **conversation-first connection app**: you meet through words — a voice note, a prompt, a real message — and the connection deepens as you talk. The heart in the logo is a speech bubble because *talking is the attraction*. Tagline: **Talk first. دل به دل راه داره.**

This design system encodes that brand into tokens, components, foundation cards, and an app UI kit so any designer or agent can produce on-brand Deltalk work.

## Source materials
Everything traces to these provided files (kept in `uploads/`):
- `Deltalk-Brand-Concept-Spec (1).md` — the foundation brand document (idea, audience, positioning, voice, color, taglines, safety). This system is built directly against it.
- `Deltalk Icon.svg` (48 × 48, 36 × 36 of ink), `Deltalk Logotype/LogoType.svg` (112 × 24) and `Deltalk Logotype/Subtitle.svg` (111 × 16) — **the complete 2026 artwork set: mark, wordmark and tagline, all outlined**. Every file in `assets/` is assembled from these three; the earlier `Deltalk-Logo.svg` and `Type=Horizontal/Vertical, Theme=Light/Dark.svg` are superseded and no longer referenced.

No codebase, Figma, fonts, or slide templates were provided. See **Caveats** for substitutions.

## Who it's for
Two audiences, one truth — *better in conversation than in a photo grid.* **Primary:** adults 35–50 (divorced, relocated, starting over) who find the swipe apps loud and humiliating. **Secondary:** 20–34s burned out on swipe culture. The brand must feel grown-up enough for a 47-year-old and alive enough for a 26-year-old — **age-neutral warmth**, never youth-slang or nightlife.

## Dark theme & contrast audit

The system ships **light and dark** themes. Dark is enabled by `data-theme="dark"` (or `.theme-dark`) on any ancestor — the preview cards and the app kit include a floating ☾/☀ toggle. Every color is a **semantic token**; the dark scope in `tokens/colors.css` overrides base tokens so both themes stay in sync, and all component states (default, hover, active, focus, disabled, selected, error) flow from them.

**The color system is now built around two explicitly separated themes** (`:root` = light, `[data-theme="dark"]` = dark, `[data-theme="light"]` = a light re-scope for forcing light under a dark ancestor). Every role has a distinct, documented value per theme — never a single shared value. Full side-by-side documentation lives in `guidelines/color-neutral.html` and `guidelines/color-semantic.html`.

**CTA rule:** every primary call-to-action — button, FAB, primary action — is `--action-cta`: `#121926` in light, `#F8FAFC` in dark, no exceptions. The brand hue never fills a primary button; it lives in the gradient, tints, and selected states.

**Brand accent stops — the ONE authoritative palette** (raw, identical in both themes, sampled from the logo file): Golden `#FFB565`, Hot Pink `#F24C9A`, Lavender `#A780FE`, Light Purple `#BB48F8`, Mid Indigo `#5852E4`, Deep Navy `#261D82`. Every other palette reference in the system now maps onto these six — the Brand Concept Spec's named colors (Apricot → `--brand-gold`; Coral/Magenta → `--brand-pink`; Lavender → `--brand-lavender`; Violet → `--brand-purple`; Indigo → `--brand-indigo`/`--brand-navy`) are aliases, not a second numerically-different set. The duplicated `-logo` literals (`--warm-apricot-logo`, `--warm-magenta-logo`, `--cool-lavender-logo`) were the same hexes typed twice and are now `var()` aliases; `--cool-lavender`'s bespoke `#896EF4` / `#B6A6FB` became ramp steps (`--brand-lavender-600` light / `-300` dark), and the ad-hoc `--warm-100/200/800` and `--cool-100/200/800/900` tints now resolve to ramp steps.

**Brand tonal ramps — 9 steps per stop** (`--brand-<name>-100 … -900`, in `tokens/colors.css`, card `guidelines/color-ramps.html`). Built by interpolating **lightness in OKLCH**, not by hex-blending toward white/black — linear hex blending bends hue and gives visually uneven steps across hues of differing saturation. Chroma tapers toward the light end and every step is gamut-mapped into sRGB. Steps are evenly spaced in OKLCH lightness within each half (100→500, 500→900) and all six ramps share the same top/bottom lightness anchors, so the same step number reads as a comparable tone in every ramp. Ramps are **theme-independent** (one hex in light and dark); per-theme surface tints stay semantic (`--accent-tint`, `--*-surface`).

**Ramp usage convention** — **500** = the color's base/reference value (the original hex, unchanged); **400–600** = the color's default flat usage (active tab state, badge icon); **700–900** = darker steps for tinted fills, hover states and subtle borders on light surfaces; **100–300** = lighter steps for text or icons placed on top of those 700–900 fills.

**THE RAMP-FIRST RULE HAS TWO DOCUMENTED EXEMPTIONS, AND TWO IS A CLASS RATHER THAN AN ANOMALY.** Both are non-brand hues that no ramp step can supply, and each was granted for a *named, different* reason — state the reason when granting a third, or do not grant it:

- **`--danger-fill` — granted because the need is FORCED.** Error must read as universally red, a red-less error is a usability failure rather than a style choice, and **no brand stop is red at all**, so the hue was a vacancy rather than a collision.
- **`--verification` #1D9BF0 — granted because the convention is EXTERNALLY FIXED.** The blue check is not ours to choose; the recognition it buys was judged worth a real cost, and unlike danger this hue was **occupied** — it collides with `--conversation-active` and is the tightest pair in the system. **This is the weaker of the two grounds and it is recorded as such.**

**A third exemption is not automatically available because there are now two.** The bar is unchanged: the hue must be unavailable from every ramp *and* the need must be either forced or externally fixed. "It would look better" has never cleared it.

> **Reach for a ramp step before reaching for `color-mix()` or a manually lightened/darkened hex — ramps are the single source of tonal variation for brand colors, keeping every screen's tints and shades consistent with each other.**

Ramps do not override the rules above them: a step never replaces `--gradient-brand` for a branded state, and never fills a primary CTA (that stays `--action-cta`).

**Brand gradient (merge decision — one identity, both themes).** The earlier light-warm / dark-cool theme-split is **cancelled**: a user only ever experiences one theme, so a theme-dependent brand color halves brand recognition. There is now **one** `--gradient-brand`, identical in light and dark:
- **`--gradient-brand`** = `#F24C9A → #BB48F8` (135°). Both stops sit mid-lightness and stay visible on `#F8FAFC` and `#121926` alike, so no per-theme tuning was needed (no stop was adjusted). White text is ~3.3–3.9:1 — fine for **large/bold text and UI shapes**, not small text. Applied to: active tab, selected chips, progress/step indicators, toggle-on track, notification badges, avatar match-rings, branded icon fills. Never a primary-CTA fill or a screen/card background.
- **`--gradient-brand-strong`** = `#C62277 → #971ECF` (135°) — **pink-600 → purple-600, ramp steps**, replacing the near-duplicate hand-darkened `#C23D7B / #963AC6`. **Contrast choice for small text on gradient:** rather than force chip/badge labels up to large-text size, a darkened variant clears AA with white (5.39:1 pink / 6.17:1 purple). Used by selected chips and notification badges.

**Exception — Chip's selected state, per-theme pair (2026-08-13).** `Chip` is the one consumer of "branded fills use the gradient identically in both themes" allowed to split by theme; everything else this rule names — switch on-track, progress, badges, avatar match-rings — stays on the shared, theme-identical gradient. Light is unchanged: `--gradient-brand-strong` + white (5.39/6.17:1). Dark switches to the undarkened `--gradient-brand` + `--brand-navy-900` ink (5.95/5.11:1). Measured reason: the darkened-for-white stops sit only 2.74–3.13:1 from dark `--surface-well` — a selected chip barely separated from an unselected one — and this pair lifts that to 4.34–5.05:1. Tokens: `--chip-selected-fill` / `--chip-selected-text`, declared at `:root` and redeclared whole inside the dark block (hazard 9 — a `var()` inside a custom property resolves where the property is declared, not where it's used, so the alias must be restated per theme to actually change). No third exception should follow from this one without an equally measured, forced reason.
- **`--gradient-celebration`** = `#FFB565 → #F24C9A` (135°). The demoted warm sweep — **celebration only** (match screen, onboarding hero, confetti). Not a UI state, accent, CTA, or background fill.

The **logo** keeps both warm and cool gradients in both themes — the one intentional place they meet.

**Semantic colors — four tokens per role, per theme** (`-fill`, `-on-fill`, `-surface`, `-text`). Fills flagged “icon/fill only” fail small-text AA on their own bg — use the `-text` token for words, the `-on-fill` token for text sitting on the fill.

## Measurement hazards — these recur, they are not one component's findings

Both were found while fitting the Avatar completion figure, but neither is about that component. **Anywhere Persian digits are fitted into a fixed space** — a count badge, a timer, a price chip, a distance chip, a progress figure — both apply.

**1 · Persian numerals are NOT equal-width in IranSans, so the widest string is not at the largest value.** `۳` is the widest glyph, which makes **`۳۳٪` the peak**, not `۹۹٪` and not `۱۰۰٪`. Sampling the decades (0 / 40 / 80 / 100) misses it entirely — that sampling produced **four successive wrong readings** of the same measurement, each one internally consistent and each one wrong. **Sweep the full range, never a sample**, and be aware the extreme value is often not the extreme *width*. Two related traps in the same family: a state that never renders should not be measured at all (`۱۰۰٪` was measured repeatedly although at 100 the slot shows a check instead), and a numeral string's width can differ from its Latin equivalent's by enough to change a fit/no-fit verdict.

**2 · Measure behind `await document.fonts.ready`, never behind a fixed delay.** A timed screenshot or a `setTimeout` can run before the webfont settles, so the measurement is taken on a **fallback face**. Measured concretely: `۳۳٪` at 9px/600 is **16.03px in IranSans and 18.03px in Tahoma** — ~6% on every figure, which was enough to move a fit verdict and to misstate an overflow count by 14 values. Related: **IRANSansMobile ships 300/400/500/700/900 — there is no 600 face**, so `--fw-semibold` is synthesised, and synthesised weight changes advance widths.

**3 · When a rule CHANGES, grep for every place the OLD rule is stated — before calling the change done.** "The new sentence was added" is **not** the same as "the change landed". Search the whole set: the **component**, its **`.d.ts`**, its **`.prompt.md`**, its **specimen card**, and **README** — then either retire each occurrence or mark it superseded with a date. This is the same defect class as the `colors.css` comment-hygiene rule below (a statement outliving the thing it described), and it recurred **four times in one session**: a duplicated `progress` JSDoc block left both the old and new figure rules alive; a slot comment still described the check as `1.62em ≈ 34%` twenty-five lines above the comment saying otherwise; the `--accent` check attribution stayed current in three files after the fill changed; and "the arc communicates completion at every size" survived in the component after being corrected in the card and README. **In every case the new rule was written correctly and the old one was simply not searched for.** The `.prompt.md` is the highest-risk copy, since it seeds new work.

**4 · Measure the rule that is in the FILE, not a hand-copied replica of it.** A harness that retypes the CSS tests the rule you *meant* to write. One turn's figures were all internally consistent and all invalid because the file had drifted one line from the harness — extract the rule from the source, or measure the mounted component.

**5 · Measure every rung, not the rungs that happen to be on the specimen card.** `xs` and `sm` were omitted from a five-rung sweep because the specimen only shows three, and the two omitted rungs were far worse than the one reported as the floor case.

**6 · A CONTRAST FIGURE IS A PROPERTY OF THE PAIR, NEVER OF THE TOKEN. Every ratio in this system names the ground it was measured against.** `--border-control` was documented as `3.43:1`; that is the figure against the **white card**. Against the **page** — the ground its own comment names as the one that matters — the same token measures `3.28:1`. One token, two grounds, two numbers, and nothing about the bare figure says which. Both clear the 3:1 floor, so nothing turned on that instance; the rule is what the instance is here for. A number written beside a token gets quoted as though it applied everywhere, which is the identical mechanism to hazard 3 and to the figure that propagated into five files after being read from a token comment instead of from the page.

Two legal forms, and no third: **state the pair** ("X on Y") where the figure is load-bearing, or **strip the figure** and let the separation tables carry it, because those get regenerated. Refreshing a wrong number to today's right one is not a fix — it resets the clock on the same defect, which is the conclusion the comment-hygiene sweep below already reached.

**WHAT THE PATTERN TURNED OUT TO BE — three mechanisms, all confirmed by this audit rather than assumed.**

**(a) A wrong figure spreads by PROXIMITY, not by reasoning.** `--text-on-cta` carries `--text-primary`'s `16.1` because it sits **ten lines below it** in `colors.css`. It carries the same wrong pair *again* in `color-neutral.html`, because there it sits **one table row away**. Nobody reasoned that white-on-a-near-black-fill and near-black-on-a-near-white-page should measure the same; the number was simply the nearest one. This is the identical mechanism that sent a figure to five files earlier in this project after it was read from a token comment instead of the page. **The corollary is a search strategy: when a figure is found wrong, check what is physically adjacent to it — the same comment block, the next declaration, the next table row — before checking what is logically related to it.**

**(b) A defect in a comment is inherited by its theme counterpart.** Dark `--border-control` repeats the light twin's defect exactly: it names the **page** and quotes the **card**'s number. Whoever wrote the dark comment copied the light one and adjusted the value, carrying the error across with it. Every one of this system's per-theme token pairs is a place this can happen. **When a figure in a light comment is found wrong, its dark twin is a suspect, not a control.**

**(c) A contradiction between two statements on one page is invisible to a file-level sweep.** `guidelines/color-neutral.html`'s caption claimed contrast was measured "against the surface each role sits on" while its column header said "vs the PAGE". The source sweep did not catch it and could not have: **each sentence is individually plausible, and grep matches strings, not disagreements.** It was found by looking at the rendered page. **A ground stated in more than one place on a page must be reconciled in the render, not the file.**

*Where that check was run, system-wide:* eight files carry contrast figures alongside a stated ground. **`color-neutral.html` was the only contradiction** — the others state grounds that are row-local and mutually consistent (`theme-accent.html` page + `--accent-tint`; `color-neutral-veils.html` white card + dark card, one per ladder step; `atoms.card.html` five grounds across five distinct pairs; `buttons.card.html` three). `color-danger-fix.html` and `logo-plus.html` name no ground in prose because their tables are **pair↔pair separation matrices** whose "pair" column *is* the ground — legitimate, and the one form of bare figure this rule permits.

**(d) I made this exact error inside the fix, which is the strongest evidence for the rule.** Correcting `--text-secondary`, I computed `5.53 / 6.99` **by hand from the hex** instead of reading it from the page, and overwrote the correct `5.54 / 6.98` in two files. The rendered page gives 5.54 / 6.98. **Arithmetic from a hex is not a measurement either** — it has the same failure mode as a copied comment, minus the excuse. Every figure in this audit was subsequently re-read from `scraps/probe-figures.html`, which resolves each token under `[data-theme]` and computes the pair live; that pass also corrected `--text-primary` page 16.85 → **16.83**, dark card 15.06 → **14.86**, `--text-disabled` 2.3 → **2.24** light and 2.6 → **2.91** dark, `--on-ink-fixed-muted` 6.9 → **6.98**, and light `--accent` 3.22 → **3.20**. **Keep that probe. A figure nobody can re-run is a claim.**

**7 · A FLEX COLUMN CONCEALS A HEIGHT SHORTFALL BY COMPRESSING ITS CHILDREN INSTEAD OF OVERFLOWING.** This is the general form of a defect found in `states.card.html`, and it has nothing to do with states. A container too short for its content will *overflow* — visibly, so you catch it — only if its children cannot shrink. Inside a flex column they can: the browser satisfies the height by squeezing whatever is most compressible, and the frame still looks correct. That card's 240px frame was 108px short of the tallest `EmptyState`, and rather than overflow it compressed the 48px CTA to **21px** — a button under half its declared height, below the touch minimum, in a specimen whose whole job is to show the component correctly. Nothing looked wrong.

Two consequences, both general: **(a)** any fixed-height container holding a flex column must have its height *measured* against its tallest real content, never estimated — `states.card.html` declares 348px for that reason; and **(b)** a component with a declared height that must not be squeezed has to defend itself, which is why `Button` sets `flex-shrink:0`. Any other fixed-shape component dropped into a flex column has the same exposure. The lesson worth keeping is the general one: **a layout defect that resolves by compression rather than overflow produces no visible symptom, so it will not be found by looking — only by measuring.**

**8 · WRITING A HAZARD DOWN DOES NOT INOCULATE YOU AGAINST IT — the person most likely to break a measurement rule is the one currently writing it.** On 2026-08-07, in the *same pass* that documented hazards 4 and 6, I published dark-theme focus figures computed by hand from `--focus-ring: var(--accent)` instead of read from the rendered page. Every dark figure was wrong (1.87 / 1.89 / 1.78 / 100-of-256 against the true 1.61 / 1.64 / 1.59 / 133-of-256), and the reason they were wrong was **the very hazard being documented two sections above**: `--ring` was theme-frozen, so the token said one thing and the page rendered another. A verifier reading the page caught it.

**This is the third instance of this same mistake in this project**, and the first two are already recorded as hazards 4 and 6. The pattern across all three: the figure was *derivable*, the derivation was short, and deriving it felt equivalent to reading it. It never is — the whole point of hazards 4 and 6 is that the file and the page disagree, and a hand derivation cannot detect the disagreement because it starts from the file.

**It then fired AGAIN, within the same turn that documented it.** Having edited `Button.jsx` and `IconButton.jsx` to compose `--_shadow` with the ring, I published "✓ Fixed" on the card and in this README **without loading the page**. The card loads the compiled `_ds_bundle.js`, which still held the pre-fix CSS, so `--_shadow` was undefined; `var(--_shadow)` with no fallback is invalid at computed-value time, which collapses the *entire* `box-shadow` declaration to `none` — and because the specimen's forced rule carried `!important`, it also suppressed each variant's own shadow. The result was a specimen showing **no rings and no halo** directly beneath a caption asserting both. The verifier caught it by reading computed styles. Two durable lessons: **a `var()` inside a shadow LIST must always carry a fallback**, because its failure mode is silent total removal rather than a visible wrong value; and **editing source is not shipping a fix** when a compiled artifact sits between the source and the page.

**Now verified on the rendered page** (`getComputedStyle`, both themes): on-media focused computes the 1dp halo (`--text-primary` at .55) *plus* the ring, secondary computes its `--border-control` inset *plus* the ring, and the dark ring is `#E481AF` — its own accent, not the frozen light one.

**It fired a FOURTH time on 2026-08-08 — and this instance is a different shape, which is what makes it worth adding.** The first three were *measurement* instances: a figure derived instead of read. This one was a **construction** instance. While writing the two-tone ring I parameterised its width as `--ring-w-in`/`--ring-w-out` so each component could override it — a construction that cannot work, for the exact reason stated in hazard 9 below, which I was in the middle of writing the warning comment for. It was caught before shipping only because the slider's override was checked; it would otherwise have rendered a plausible 2px and looked correct.

**The observation to keep: writing about a hazard puts you in precisely the material where it applies, with your attention on the prose rather than on the code.** The rule was known, written, and being written *about*, and the instinct still produced the construction it forbids. Three measurement instances and one construction instance, **all four in the moment of documenting**. Treat the documentation pass itself as a high-risk edit, not a safe one — a hazard section is written in the same file, on the same values, by someone whose working memory is full of prose.

**9 · A `var()` INSIDE A CUSTOM PROPERTY RESOLVES WHERE THE PROPERTY IS DECLARED, NOT WHERE IT IS USED — so a `:root` alias cannot be overridden downstream, and the failure is a plausible value rather than an error.** This has now produced three silent failures in this project — the `--on-media-*` family froze to the light theme, `--ring` froze to the light theme, and `--ring-w-in` would have frozen to 2px — so it is recorded as a mechanism rather than as three incidents.

The rule: when a custom property's value contains `var(--x)`, the substitution happens at computed-value time **on the element carrying the declaration**. What inherits downstream is the already-substituted result. Nothing further down — not a `[data-theme]` subtree, not a component's own rule — can change it, because there is no `var()` left to re-resolve.

**Why it is worse than an ordinary bug: there is no error state.** The declaration is valid, the cascade is working as specified, and the rendered value is a real value from the right family — the light theme's colour, or the default width. It looks like a decision. It is only detectable by reading the *rendered* value in the scope that was supposed to override it, which is why this hazard and hazards 4/6/8 keep appearing together.

**Two corollaries, both now enforced in this project:**

1. **An alias of a theme-dependent token is redeclared in every theme block** — `:root`, `[data-theme="light"]` and `[data-theme="dark"]` — alongside the tokens it aliases. This is the three-site rule the `--on-media-*` family follows in `colors.css` and the two `--glow-*` tokens follow in `shadows.css`.
2. **A value meant to be overridden per component is passed as a LITERAL, never composed through an alias.** If two widths are needed, that is two tokens (`--ring` and `--ring-wide`), not one token plus a width variable. The moment a per-component knob is expressed as a `var()` inside a shared property, it stops being a knob.

**The inverse also holds and is easy to miss:** a token that composes *no* theme-dependent `var()` should be declared **once**, and repeating it per theme is dead weight that can drift. When `--ring` went two-tone and theme-independent, its per-theme duplicates were removed for that reason. The three-site rule is a consequence of the mechanism, not a style — apply it exactly where the mechanism applies.

**So the operational rule, stated as an instruction:** any published contrast figure is read from the rendered page under the theme it claims, with `getComputedStyle`, in the same pass that publishes it. **Not derived, not copied, not recomputed from a token's declared value — including when you are the one writing the rule that says so.** Documenting a hazard is the moment of *highest* risk for it, because you are handling exactly the material the hazard applies to while your attention is on the prose rather than on the measurement.

## Comment hygiene — never cite a value you don't own

A comment may state **its own** token's measurements: its value, and its contrast on the page, card or surface. Those can only go stale in the same edit that changes the token, so they are self-describing and safe.

A comment must **not** cite a neighbour's hex, nor a margin against a neighbour. **Name the neighbour by TOKEN NAME and state that separation was verified — never the number.**

Where a margin **is** the justification for a value existing, it does not live in a comment at all: it goes where it is regenerated and re-measured, alongside the state-colour separation table below. *A justification nobody re-runs is a claim, not a measurement.*

**The pattern that produced this rule, recorded because it is a standing hazard and not a one-time cleanup.** A sweep of `tokens/colors.css` (2026-08-01) found 128 comment lines citing a figure, across 33 distinct hexes. Six were stale, and the split was total:

- **Every stale entry was a cross-token claim. Not one self-describing figure had drifted** — all 14 luminance measurements checked out to two decimals.
- **Every drift was in HUE.** `--nav-active`↔`--accent` was written as 60° and measured **71.98°** (light) / 56° vs **75.87°** (dark); `--conversation-active`↔`--info-fill` 43° vs **47.93°** (dark); `--accent`↔`--danger-fill` 37° vs **33.73°**; `--danger-fill`↔`--premium` 33° vs **27.03°**.
- **The cause is the re-derivations themselves.** Each of those tokens was moved onto a ramp step to hit a *luminance* target — page contrast, AA on the dark surface. Luminance was therefore re-measured and stayed true; **hue moved as an unmeasured side effect and nobody re-ran the neighbours.** The next re-derivation will do the same thing unless the margins live somewhere that gets re-measured.
- The drifted figures were **stripped, not corrected** — writing today's numbers back into a comment just resets the clock on the same defect.
- Two hexes were live-stale rather than historical: `--conversation-active` quoted `--info-fill` as `#3B4CC0` (light) and `#9F8BF8` (dark), both superseded. The light one had already misled a reading of the system. Thirteen other non-live hexes are correctly **historical** ("the old #FEAC6A", "was #C43D6E") — those are records, not claims, and are left alone.

## Colour labelling rule (guidelines cards)

Every place a hex appears in a guidelines card, it carries its **exact source**. One rule, no bare hexes and no bare family names:

- **Brand ramp step** → labelled `brand-<family>-<step>` (e.g. `brand-pink-600`, `brand-gold-300`) — never just "pink" or the hex alone.
- **Neutral ladder step** → labelled `neutral-<step>` (e.g. `neutral-700`).
- **Deliberate non-ramp exception** → labelled `non-ramp — <reason>` (e.g. "non-ramp — needs universal red", "non-ramp — reserved green"), with §Reconciliation rulings as the pointer for why. Rendered in the warning colour so exceptions are visually countable.
- **One-off tuned variant** matching no step exactly → labelled `tuned from brand-<family>-<step>` (e.g. the dark accent `#E481AF`, tuned from `brand-pink-500`).

The six brand stops are labelled `= brand-<family>-500` on the accent-stops card, since each stop *is* its ramp's step 500.

## Size scale & declared heights

`tokens/sizes.css` holds element **dimensions** (the size of a thing) as against `spacing.css` (the gap between things). Steps are named by pixel value, all on the 4px grid: marks `8 / 12 / 16`, controls `20 / 24 / 28 / 32 / 36 / 40 / 44 / 48 / 52 / 56 / 60 / 64 / 72 / 76`, plus `--size-112` (Avatar xl).

### The rule (adopted)

**A fixed-shape component declares its height.** Any pill, capsule, dot, count badge, tab item or segment must set `height` from a `--size-*` step, centre its content with `display:inline-flex` + `align-items:center`, and take **horizontal padding only**, from a spacing token. Vertical padding is never what produces a height. Components that must grow with their content — cards, message bubbles, list rows, text areas — are exempt, but their padding still comes from spacing tokens.

**Root cause this closes.** `Badge` rendered at **23px** because it declared no height at all: 5px padding × 2 + line-height 1 × `--text-caption` 13px. Nobody chose 23; it was computed, and it would have silently changed again the next time the type scale moved. A size scale alone would not have prevented that — the scale plus a *declared*-height rule does.

**Comment convention.** The height line carries `/* h: --size-24 declared */`. A fixed-shape component with no height and no marker is a defect. There is no linter for it — `_adherence.oxlintrc.json` is compiler-generated and cannot carry a hand-written rule — so the record is this section: every covered component, its selector and its rung, verified by measuring the live client rect in both themes. Adding a pill means adding a row here. The card `guidelines/spacing-sizes.html` stays a plain specimen sheet: the scale, the rule, and every fixed shape at true scale in light and dark.

## PRODUCT VOCABULARY — the words themselves are decisions

### ⚠ RETIRED TERM — «پسند» → «لایک» (2026-08-04, all 11 occurrences)

**«لایک» is the term. «پسند» and its verb forms («بپسندید», «پسندیدم») are retired and must not be reintroduced.** Approved forms: «لایک» (the control label) · «لایک کردم» · «لایک کنید» · «لایک کنه».

**The rule this sweep exists to state: a default string inside a component is user-facing text, not configuration.** `EmptyState.noMatches` carried the retired word as a hardcoded default, so every screen that mounted `EmptyState` without passing its own copy inherited it. Defaults must be swept in the same pass as the app, never after it — a vocabulary sweep that starts in the app and stops at the app leaves the component defaults sitting there ready to reinject the retired word at the next refresh. The built bundle counts too: it carries its own copy of every default string, so a source fix is only half the sweep until the bundle is rebuilt and re-grepped.

**Scope of the sweep:** `components/feedback/EmptyState.jsx` (the `noMatches` default — also de-formalised: «همدیگه رو لایک کنید» over «همدیگر را بپسندید»), `_ds_bundle.js`, `components/forms/buttons.card.html` (one button label + seven `label=` props), `components/feedback/overlays.card.html`.

### ⚠ RETIRED TERM — «همدل» → «مچ» (2026-08-02, all 12 occurrences)

**«همدل» is retired everywhere and must not be reintroduced.** Two reasons, and the second is the decisive one:
1. **A product term cannot have two names.** A "colloquial contexts only" split would mean the user reads one word in settings and a different one in chat — the same inconsistency, better hidden.
2. **«همدل» is the name of an existing dating app in the Iranian market.** A recurring product word that evokes a competitor's brand is not a style question.

**Approved forms** (use these verbatim; each is colloquial second-person, per the voice rules): «مچ شدید!» · «مچ جدید» · «هنوز مچی نداری» · «مچ‌های تازه» · «تا وقتی مچ نشید».

**Rejected — do not write these:** ⚠ «این مچ پایان یافت» and «مچ شما برداشته می‌شود». Both are formal-literary where the voice is warm and colloquial, **and there is no match-expiry feature at all**, so no string should express one. Where an unmatch genuinely has to be described, the shipped phrasings are «دیگه مچ نیستید» (`ChatState.unmatched`) and «دیگر مچ نیستید» (the block confirmation).

**Scope of the sweep:** `ChatState`, `EmptyState`, `PermissionCard`, `ChatListItem`, `settings.card.html`, `safety.card.html`, `voice-typing.card.html`, `guidelines/color-gradient.html`, and three UI-kit files. **`color-gradient.html` was included deliberately** even though it only demonstrates colour: card copy gets read and copied by whoever uses the card, so a specimen carrying a retired term teaches it.

**The core idea — warm ↔ cool duality.** Two temperatures meeting at a clean seam (never blended into mush): **Warm** (apricot→coral→magenta) = the user, the reach, feeling, primary actions — دل. **Cool** (indigo→violet→lavender) = the other person, the reply, calm structure, "conversation active" — talk. These carry *meaning*; don't scatter them decoratively.

- **Color.** Two explicitly separated themes (light/dark), six raw brand accent stops, and the signature **Deltalk gradient** — now **one theme-independent** `--gradient-brand` (`#F24C9A → #BB48F8`), with `--gradient-brand-strong` for small text and `--gradient-celebration` (the demoted warm sweep) for celebration moments only. Discipline: **one hero-gradient moment per screen/slide** — everything else is flat surface or tint. Neutrals are a **blue-gray** ramp (bg `#F8FAFC` / `#121926`), never pure warm paper. Semantic status colors are kept human, not clinical. Tokens in `tokens/colors.css`.
- **Type.** Primary family is **IRANSans Mobile (NoEn)** — the brand's Persian typeface, self-hosted (`assets/fonts/`) and used throughout the whole system and every component (UI, body, and headings, weights 300/400/500/700/900). **Morabba** is kept as an optional display accent for hero titles. Latin (the "Deltalk" logotype only) = Hanken Grotesk. Mono = JetBrains Mono. **Persian-script rules are baked into the tokens:** letter-spacing is **zero** everywhere, line-heights are **generous** (1.75 body, up to 1.9 long-form), ultra-thin weights are avoided at small sizes, and Persian numerals render in RTL. Scale + tokens in `tokens/typography.css`.
- **Spacing & layout.** 4px base grid. Semantic layer adopted from the spec (merge §3): screen gutter **20px**, section gap 24, card padding 16, card-to-card gap 12, element gap 8, stacked-input gap 16. Fixed elements: top bar (sticky), bottom nav. Tokens in `tokens/spacing.css`.
- **Dimensions & targets.** Full-width primary CTA height **52** (`--control-h-button`), input height **48** (`--control-h-input`), minimum touch target **48dp** (`--touch-min`, Android Material), focus/error ring **2dp** (`--border-focus-w`) in `--focus-ring`.
- **Inputs — the ONE FRAME rule.** A field renders exactly **one** bordered/rounded surface: the wrapper. It owns the surface (`--surface-input`), a **1dp** `--border`, `--radius-input`, the control height and the horizontal padding. The inner `<input>`/`<textarea>` is **naked** — transparent, `border:none`, no outline, no shadow, `border-radius:0`, no padding — carrying only typography and text color, flexing to fill. **Every state lives on the wrapper** via `:focus-within`, never on the inner control: rest (1dp `--border`), focus (1.5dp `--focus-ring`), filled (rest + `--text-body`), error (1.5dp `--danger` + `--danger-text` helper), disabled (`--surface-well` + `--text-disabled`, no ring). Never nest two bordered/rounded surfaces in one field.
  **Affixes** are plain content, never boxed slots: the `+۹۸` prefix is text inside the same wrapper, separated by a single 1dp `--border` divider (full height less 8px, 12px clear either side); leading/trailing icons are bare icons. The prefix must not change the control height or introduce a second rounded shape.
  **RTL + digits:** every field is RTL — text right-aligned, caret starting at the right edge, placeholder right-aligned, leading icons at the RTL start (right). Name, city, job, prompt answers, search, composer, textarea and the select trigger all follow this.
  **The phone field is the ONE exception: it is fully LTR.** `direction:ltr` is set on the **wrapper**, not just the inner input, so the whole control reads left-to-right: a **fixed `+98`** text affix → 1dp divider → number entry filling the remaining width, `text-align:left` so the caret sits immediately after the divider and typing grows rightward. The prefix is **static text with no chevron and no country picker** — Deltalk is Iran-only, so `+98` never changes and a disclosure affordance would imply a choice that doesn't exist. It uses **Latin digits** rather than Persian numerals (placeholder `912 345 6789`), matching how numbers are dictated and arrive by SMS. Its **label, helper text and error message stay RTL and right-aligned** — only the control is LTR. This exception is scoped to the phone control and must not leak into any other field.
  Applies to the whole family: single-line text, textarea, phone-with-prefix, search/composer, select trigger, and OTP (each box is its own single frame; the active box takes the 2dp focus ring — never an inner box).
- **Numerals: Persian (`۰–۹`) everywhere, with two scoped exceptions.** The **phone field** and the **OTP boxes** render **Latin** digits — both are code entry transcribed from an SMS, so they must match the numerals the message itself shows. Everything else stays Persian: ages, distances, timestamps, counters, prices, and the OTP resend countdown (that is prose, not entry).

The slot sets no font-size. **Two bugs came from moving it:** on the **pill**, its own `em` height/padding resolved against the font-size the clamp was setting — circular — pinning them at 15px/5px on every rung so the pill was identical on a 112dp and a 52dp avatar; on the **slot**, the check inherited the clamp and silently shrank 37–40% while ceasing to scale consistently.
  - **SUPERSEDED 2026-08-02 — do not reinstate:** *"the figure itself always stays in text — it is never drawn inside the ring."* That rule is withdrawn, along with the caption beneath the avatar. The arc sits outside the photo, so it coexists with `ring` rather than competing with it, and it mirrors in RTL like every other progress indicator.
- **Danger has TWO weights, chosen by REVERSIBILITY.** Solid `Button variant="destructive"` = **irreversible** (delete the account, a destructive dialog's confirm). `tone="danger"` on `secondary`/`ghost` = **reversible but consequential** (leave the conversation, log out): the label moves to `--danger-text`, the outline stays `--border-control`, so the button's weight is unchanged and only its meaning is coloured. **Solid variants ignore `tone`** — the class isn't emitted for them, so two danger treatments can never stack.
- **A chevron is a NAVIGATION PROMISE.** On `SettingsRow`, `variant` only sets the chevron's default (`chevron`/`value`/`destructive` show one, `switch` doesn't); `chevron={false}` withdraws it for a row that **opens a dialog or fires an action** instead of going to a screen, so the promise is never made falsely.
- **`IconButton` solid carries NO elevation — do not reinstate it as a "missing" default.** A census of all 31 mounts found **zero** genuine consumers of `--shadow-md` and two composer sends overriding it back down to `--shadow-sm`. A control that genuinely floats adds its own elevation deliberately; the dependency runs from the consumer to the variant, not the other way.
- **A control under 48dp keeps its visual size and gains a transparent `::after` inset to `--size-48` — inside the COMPONENT, never as a rule the consumer must remember.** Applies to `Button` sm (40) and `IconButton` 36/40; 48 and above meet it outright and get no inset. The older "sm is a dense-row exception needing 48dp of clear space around it" wording is **withdrawn** — a hit-area guarantee that depends on every call site remembering is not a guarantee, and it had also drifted to a size (38dp) that never existed.
- **`IconButton` sizes are PIXEL-NAMED** (`36 | 40 | 48 | 52`), matching the `--size-*` scale, with `sm | md | lg` kept as aliases for 36/48/52. The role names had stopped describing anything — md→lg was a 4px step while sm→md was 12. **36 and 40 keep their visual size and reach the 48dp minimum through a transparent inset**, inside the component, so no consumer can forget it.
- **`IconButton variant="onMedia"`** for a control sitting on a user photo: theme-**independent**, because there is no theme surface to answer to — and the plate is **OPAQUE**, which is the whole point. `--ink-fixed` fill + 1dp `--ink-fixed-edge` ring + `--shadow-on-photo` + `--on-ink-fixed` glyph = **17.61:1 over any photograph, bright or dark**, because the ratio no longer depends on pixels nobody controls. Hover is a **lift** to `--ink-fixed-hover` (8.53:1), not more opacity: an opaque plate has no opacity to step. **It previously bound `--veil-on-media-medium` (.20) + `backdrop-filter: blur(6px)` and measured 1.00:1 — a white glyph on a plate composited to white — over any ground brighter than ~48% grey (sky, snow, sand, a white wall).** `AgeChip --onmedia` and `DistanceChip --onmedia` were the same defect at a thinner .16 and moved to the same construction in the same pass. **`VerificationBadge onPhoto` was moved onto the same construction in the same pass, because it was the component the rest of the system was being told to copy while itself failing.** Only its **mark** was ever theme-independent; its `lg`/`sm` pill followed the theme and measured **1.12:1** fill / 1.63:1 ring on a pinned bright ground in light theme, surviving on `--shadow-on-photo` alone (16.89:1 in dark — a container that flips over content that does not flip cannot hold a ratio). `onPhoto` now takes the opaque plate and measures 17.61:1 on both grounds; the pending label on it is `--on-ink-fixed-muted` at 6.98:1; `mini` is untouched and still bare. **This is NOT a revert of the 2026-08-02 decision that made the pill theme-following** — that ruling's reason was "a dark chip on a light PAGE for a fact that is not an alert" and it still governs every on-surface placement. `onPhoto` is the one placement with no theme surface, which is why only it changed. **And the `tokens/colors.css` ring ruling is satisfied, not overridden:** that ruling says an `--ink-fixed` fill cannot itself be the boundary on a dark ground (it equals the dark page at 1.00:1) so the capsule must be defined by a 1dp ring clearing 3:1 — it *presupposes* the opaque ink fill rather than forbidding it, and `--ink-fixed-edge` measures 4.12:1 dark / 4.50:1 bright. **Cite the construction, not a component.**
- **`PresenceIndicator --onmedia`** bound its ring to a bare `rgba(0,0,0,.35)` literal — a fourth untracked value for a boundary the system already had a token for. It now binds `--ink-fixed`. It is a complementary pair, not a single boundary: on a dark ground the ring merges (1.05:1) and the dot carries (5.62:1); on a bright ground the ring carries (17.61:1). Dot-vs-ring is 5.25:1.
- **CONTRAST FIGURES ARE READ FROM THE RENDERED PAGE — never from a token comment, a neighbouring card, or another ruling.** A number copied from documentation is documentation, not a measurement. Recorded because it happened here: the `16.4:1` in `--on-ink-fixed`'s comment was cited as the measured on-media ratio and **propagated to five files** before the rendered value (17.61:1) was read off the loaded stylesheet. The same pass also held up `VerificationBadge onPhoto` as a working precedent for three prompts without measuring it — it was at 1.12:1. Both errors were made by reading documentation instead of the page. **A source edit is also not a measurement until `_ds_bundle.js` is rebuilt:** the cards load the bundle, so an unrebuilt bundle renders the old rule while the caption asserts the new figure. `blur()` changes no luminance, so a blur is never a boundary; "give the translucent plate its own ground" was measured failing twice here (`VerificationBadge mini`, `Avatar progress` pill). **`--veil-on-media-*` is now legal only on a surface the system itself controls** (`ChatBubble` sent-quote .16, `VoiceNote` play .20 — both on the sent bubble, neither on a photo), never behind a glyph or text on user media.
- **ONE icon set, one file: `components/core/icons.jsx`**, built on `dsIcon` and published as `window.DeltalkIcons` when the bundle loads. It replaced two line-for-line-identical copies (`ui_kits/deltalk-app/Icons.jsx`, `ui_kits/deltalk-ui-kit/kit-icons.js`) that exported the **same global from two paths** and so could drift unseen — the same failure as the 42px/40px play buttons. **No shim was left**: a shim keeps the second definition alive. Specimen cards read the same set; none hand-writes an `<svg>` for a glyph the set already has.
- **Every DS glyph comes from the `dsIcon` factory** (`components/core/dsIcon.jsx`), never a hand-written `<svg>`.
- **A DIVIDED LIST IS WHAT STACKED `SettingsRow`s LOOK LIKE — and the divider is STRUCTURAL, not a prop.** Adjacent-sibling CSS inside the component (`.dt-setrow + .dt-setrow{border-top:1px solid var(--border)}`) draws the hairline **only between two rows**, so the first row gets no rule above it and the last none below, automatically. The alternative that was rejected: a `divider` boolean the parent sets `false` on the last row. A React child cannot inspect its siblings, so a row genuinely *cannot* know it is last — the prop would have pushed that bookkeeping onto every consumer, which is exactly the class of consumer-memory rule the icon-factory and 48dp-inset sweeps removed. The sibling rule cannot be got wrong because there is nothing to get right. **`divider={false}` exists only to WITHDRAW the hairline above a row set deliberately apart** (a destructive action separated from the group) — a design decision, never "is last" bookkeeping. A **dedicated list-wrapper component was also rejected**: it would do with a new component what a sibling selector already does, and it would make the divided list opt-in, letting the specimen and the product diverge again. It becomes the right answer only if divided lists of *mixed* content appear; audited at the time of the decision, every stacked-row container in the app kit held contiguous `SettingsRow`s only, with section headings outside the container.
  - **The divider is `--border`, NOT `--border-control`** — it is a decorative separator inside a card whose surface already carries the boundary, which is the distinction the two tokens draw.
  - **The container is `Card pad="none"`** (zero padding + `overflow:hidden`, added for this): rows run full-bleed and corner-clip to the card radius. Nothing should hand-roll a `surface-card` + `radius` + `overflow:hidden` wrapper for a list again. `directional` is a **position in the signature**, so an icon cannot be defined without answering whether it mirrors in RTL. This is structural, not a convention: the app kit's identical factory produced zero misses across 30 glyphs, while the one component that hand-rolled its own helper shipped the send arrow **unmirrored** in an RTL-first app. Vertical chevrons are NOT directional.
- **`IconButton` shape carries meaning, and the DEFAULT is the rounded square.** `shape="square"` (`--radius-button`, the default) = a utility control in the app chrome whose label didn't fit: search, filter, settings, back, three-dot, close. It shares `Button`'s radius so it reads as part of the button family. `shape="circle"` (`--radius-pill`) is an explicit opt-in and means the control floats over content or belongs to the avatar family: the Like/Pass pair, the small on-photo and on-prompt hearts, the composer's send button. The frequent case is the default so that the shape is right when nobody thinks about it; the rarer case has to be written down. `MessageInput`'s inline mic/attach follow the same rule (square); only its send button is round.
- **Radius: reach for the ROLE, not the step.** Five roles — `--radius-button` 16, `--radius-input` 12 (text field, textarea, select trigger, OTP box), `--radius-card` 20, `--radius-sheet` 28 (bottom sheets, dialogs, large overlays — added in this pass; sheets had been using raw `--radius-xl`), `--radius-chip` pill. The raw ramp (`xs…pill`) now carries **no role notes**: `--radius-md` used to claim "inputs, chips" while inputs are 12px and chips are a pill, which is why components drifted onto the ramp. **Inputs were split between 12px and 14px — 12px wins**, and the whole input family is on `--radius-input`.
- **Spacing: reach for the ROLE, not the number.** Six semantic tokens sit on the 4px ramp — `--gutter-screen` 20 (screen horizontal padding), `--gap-section` 24 (between major sections), `--pad-card` 16 (card interior), `--gap-card` 12 (card-to-card / list rows), `--gap-stack` 16 (stacked form fields), `--gap-inline` 8 (elements within a row). A role can be re-tuned system-wide; a raw `--space-N` cannot. Raw steps stay legal only for genuine one-offs (optical nudges, decorative offsets) where no role applies. **`Card`'s default padding was 24 — off the documented 16** — and is now `--pad-card`; the old 24 is still reachable as `pad="lg"`, and `pad="sm"` steps down to 12.
- **STATE-COLOUR SEPARATION — `--accent` / `--danger-fill` / `--premium`.** All three can appear on one screen (focused field, invalid field, premium affordance), so each pair must differ by **≥1.5:1 luminance OR ≥30° of hue**. Two axes are needed because in dark all three must stay light enough to clear AA on `#121926`, which compresses luminance and leaves hue as the only axis; in light the accent is light while danger/premium are dark, so luminance does the work. Margins — LIGHT: accent↔danger 1.69:1 · accent↔premium 2.09:1 · danger↔premium 37°. DARK: accent↔danger 34° · accent↔premium 1.74:1 · danger↔premium 1.87:1 + 33°. Dark had **no** guard before: danger `#F26E86` and premium `#F583B4` were 1.10:1 and 1.09:1 from the accent. `--warm-coral` was **deleted** — it failed both axes against all three and no component referenced it.
- **`ConfirmDialog` has ONE media slot, and `icon` is shorthand for it.** Everything above the title renders through `media`; `icon` resolves into it internally, so there is a single code path and no precedence rule — `media` wins if both are set, and every existing halo consumer is byte-identical. **A dialog about a PERSON leads with the face:** `media={<Avatar size="xl"/>}` at **112dp**, which is **1.87× the 60dp halo** and the rung Profile's hero already uses, so a face is the same size everywhere a face is the point. `cancelLabel=""` drops the second button for dialogs that **acknowledge rather than ask** — the scrim still dismisses.
  - **`mediaBadge` owns POSITION AND GROUND; the node owns only its own fill.** The overlap geometry lives in the slot because geometry composed at each call site drifts — every consumer eyeballs a different offset. ⚠ **The backing lives there for a sharper reason: a badge node cannot know it has been dropped onto user photography, and the slot can.** Leaving legibility to the node makes every consumer re-derive a boundary from context it cannot see. So pass the **bare glyph**, unwrapped.
  - **The 112dp figure, kept from the card's moved paragraph:** the avatar is **1.87×** the 60dp icon halo, and the badge is 36dp with a 20dp glyph — **56% of the circle, the same glyph-to-container ratio the halo uses** (28 in 60), so the two media forms are proportioned alike even though one is a face and one is an icon.
  - **Measured — a `--gradient-brand` heart alone fails both LIGHT avatar tones**, the same profile as the blue rosette: pink stop **2.35:1** on `#E8D5C4` and **2.88:1** on cream `#F4EDE3`, purple stop **2.74:1** on `#E8D5C4`; both dark tones pass (3.14–5.30:1). A **white silhouette outline was measured and rejected again** (1.42:1 / 1.16:1) — it separates only where the mark already passes, which is now the third time that trap has been priced. The shipped backing is the **complementary pair**: `--ink-fixed` fill + 1dp `--ink-fixed-edge` + a 2dp `--on-ink-fixed` ring — ink carries the light tones (12.36:1 / 15.15:1), the ring carries the dark ones (12.32:1 / 17.79:1), 17.61:1 between them, **all four clear 3:1**. The heart reads 5.16:1 on the ink.
  - **⚠ THIS IS NOT A THIRD SOLUTION — it is the same rule, affordable at a size where `VerificationBadge` mini could not afford it.** Mini dropped its container because at a 24dp footprint the container forced the mark to 14dp, under the 2px feature floor. **That cost was SIZE-SPECIFIC and does not transfer:** here the badge is 36dp on a 112dp avatar with a 20dp glyph, nowhere near a floor, so the container is free and the ring comes back with it. **Do not "harmonise" this with mini by stripping the backing** — mini's answer is a concession to 24dp, not a system preference.
- **`--scrim` / `--scrim-media`.** One token for modal backdrops (`BottomSheet`, `ConfirmDialog`, `Dialog` each had their own literal for the same concept) and a separate near-black ramp for gradient overlays on user photos, which is intentionally theme-independent.
- **Jalali math lives in `components/forms/jalali.jsx`** — a shared internal helper with no sibling `.d.ts`, so the compiler treats it as a plain module instead of registering a second date component. `ShamsiDatePicker` (calendar) was **retired**; `DateOfBirthFieldGroup` (typed entry) is the only date-of-birth control.
- **`--border-control` vs `--border`.** `--border-control` (light `#828B9C` · dark `#6E7789`; its ratio differs per ground and is tabled, not quoted here) is the **1dp outline for any control whose fill equals the surface behind it** — the one-frame input family at rest (`Input`, `PhoneInput`, `Select`, `OtpInput`, `MessageInput`), `Button variant="secondary"`, `IconButton variant="outline"`. For those the border is the control's *only* boundary, so it must clear the 3:1 non-text minimum. `--border` (light `#C3CBD6` 1.64:1 · dark `#454D5A` 1.82:1) is **decorative only** — card outlines, dividers, list separators — where the surface itself already provides the boundary. Never use `--border` on a field.
- **`--text-subtle` must clear 4.5:1 on `--surface-well`, not just the card.** Placeholders, helper text and affixes sit on wells (the composer, disabled fields), which is the tightest pairing: light `#636E82` (4.57 well / 5.14 card / 4.92 page), dark `#828C9E` (4.98 / 4.59 / 5.19). Both stay a visible step below `--text-secondary`. Earlier values (`#697488` / `#7A8496`) failed on wells in their own theme.
- **Theme-scoping wrappers must re-declare inherited `color`/`background`.** A `[data-theme]` wrapper re-scopes the *custom properties* correctly, but `color:var(--text-primary)` set on `body` already **resolved** to the light literal, and that computed value inherits straight past the re-scope — so text inside a dark panel renders in light ink. Every specimen card's theme wrapper (`.tbox` / `.themebox` / `.panel`) therefore sets its own `color:var(--text-primary)`. Same root cause as the alias note below: a `var()` is substituted where it is *declared*, not where it is *used*.
- **Theme-scoped alias caution.** A custom property declared only on `:root` as `var(--other)` resolves its substitution *at :root*, and that already-computed value inherits — so a `[data-theme]` **subtree** keeps the light values. Any alias that must re-resolve per theme has to be re-declared inside each theme scope (the chat-bubble aliases are).
- **Online/offline is a separate semantic set** — `--success` (online), `--warning` (recently active), `--text-subtle` (offline) — so there is exactly one online color across avatars, chat rows, swipe cards, profile sheets and `PresenceIndicator`.
- **`--focus-ring` applies ONLY to focus states.** Never a fill, track, badge or dot. ⚠ **`--ring` (the focus box-shadow) is no longer derived from it** (2026-08-08): the ring is two-tone and theme-independent, built from `--ring-ink`/`--ring-edge`. `--focus-ring` now carries focus only where a *bordered* control expresses it by recolouring its own border — `Select` and `Input`. See "Focus ring — two-tone".
- **Focus ring is the BRAND PINK — one meaning, two theme values.** Focus is a *state signal*, so its identity must stay constant across themes: `--focus-ring` is pink in both, never purple in dark, and never the gradient (a ring is always a solid color). It is defined once as a semantic token and never overridden inside a component.
  - **Light `#F24C9A`** — the brand pink as-is; it sits well on `#F8FAFC`.
  - **Dark `#E481AF`** — deliberately *not* the same hex. On the dark blue-gray surface `#F24C9A` reads neon and glowing, which breaks the brand's "warm, not hot" rule. Same hue (332°), desaturated 87% → 65% and lightened 62% → 70%, so it reads as a calm, confident signal rather than a highlighter outline — unmistakably the same pink, tuned for a dark surface.
  Contrast against the adjacent field surface: **7.4:1** light (on `#FFFFFF`) and **6.0:1** dark (on `#1D2432`) — both far above the 3:1 non-text minimum, so no lightening was needed.
  The ring sits on the **wrapper** via `:focus-within` at **2dp** (`--border-focus-w`) against the 1dp rest border, making the rest→focus jump clearly perceptible. **Error wins over focus:** a field that is both invalid and focused shows the `--danger` border, not the pink ring. Being a token change, it propagates to every focusable control — text inputs, phone, textarea, search, composer, select trigger and OTP boxes.
- **Text color tokens are `--text-primary` / `--text-secondary` / `--text-subtle` / `--text-disabled`.** The primary one is deliberately **not** called `--text-body`: that name is the 1rem base **font-size** in `tokens/typography.css`. The two collided — same name, one a length and one a color, with typography imported last — so `color:var(--text-body)` computed to `1rem`, was dropped as invalid, and every field and label silently fell back to inherited black instead of the token. Use `--text-body` **only** as a font-size; `--text-primary` for color.
- **Corners.** Soft and rounded everywhere — nothing sharp/clinical. Cards `20px`, sheets `28px`, buttons `--radius-button` 16 — **not** pill. **Fully round is avatars and the floating action controls only** (the Like/Pass pair, the on-photo/on-prompt hearts, the composer's send button). *Corrected 2026-07-29: this line previously read "buttons/avatars fully pill", which is where the wrongly circular `IconButton` came from — every screen inherited a circle because the written rule said so.* Speech bubbles are rounded except the tail corner (RTL-mirrored). `tokens/radii.css`.
- **Backgrounds.** Light `#F8FAFC` or dark `#121926`. No busy patterns or aggressive gradients — the gradient is a reserved hero device. Photography is warm-toned; **photos start soft-blurred and clarify as a conversation builds** (the signature reveal). Full-bleed imagery only behind gradient scrims.
- **Elevation.** Soft, **warm-tinted** shadows (tinted toward the ink), never hard grey drops. Two colored glows (`--glow-warm`, `--glow-cool`) reserved for the one hero action. `tokens/shadows.css`.
- **Cards.** Warm-paper raised surface, `20px` radius, soft `shadow-md`, 1px hairline border (`--line`). Interactive cards lift `-2px` on hover. Variants: raised / flat (well) / cool (dark) / gradient (hero).
- **Borders.** Hairline blue-gray lines (`--border` `#C3CBD6` light / `#454D5A` dark), deliberately one step darker so they stay visible — no sub-pixel hairlines (minimum 1dp, per merge §5). Inputs use a 1.5dp inset ring that turns the focus color on focus.
- **Motion — the UNFOLD, never the swipe.** Nothing swipes away; things ease open and reveal (`--ease-out-soft`, `dt-unfold`, photo `dt-unblur`). Gentle, human durations (140/240/420/560ms). The **three-dots typing indicator** is the core micro-motion and a recurring brand device (loading, section breaks, build beats).
- **Hover / press.** Hover = subtle darken (warm→warm-700) or a `warm-50` tint / card lift. Press = gentle **shrink** (`scale(0.97)`), never a hard bounce.
- **Transparency & blur.** Used purposefully: scrims over photos, `blur()` on unrevealed avatars/photos, a soft backdrop-blur behind dialogs. Not decorative glassmorphism.

### System audit (state)
All 49 components compile and are exposed on the namespace. **Removed on request:** the Navigation group (`BottomNav`, `TopAppBar`, `SearchBar`), the Monetization group (`Paywall`, `PlanCard`, `PremiumBadge`, `ConsumableCounter`), and the **swipe stack** (`SwipeCard`, `SwipeActions`, plus the "Swipe direction — RTL convention" card) — nine components whose structure did not match the real app screens. The swipe stack went because Discover is a vertically-scrollable single card: drag gestures, directional overlays and rewind/super-like/boost belong to a paradigm this product rejected. They were deleted outright (`.jsx`, `.d.ts`, `.prompt.md` and their specimen cards) rather than merely un-carded, so a stale shape cannot be re-imported from the bundle by mistake. The app kit's tab bar is now a small inline component owned by `ui_kits/deltalk-app/App.jsx`, built to the current navigation spec and the real consumer of `--nav-active`. Light + dark are automatic: every component draws only from semantic tokens, so `data-theme="dark"` re-skins them with no per-component work. State coverage lives in each component's props (drag/delivery/upload/empty/error/loading/selected/disabled as applicable). No hardcoded brand hex remains in component logic — the few literal values are neutral overlay scrims (`rgba(8,6,16,…)` gradients on photos) and pure white/black on media, which are intentionally theme-independent.
**Feedback group cards are complete** — three cards cover all nine components: `feedback.card.html` (the combined sampler, with the tweakable Snackbar treatment), `overlays.card.html` (Dialog · ConfirmDialog info/destructive · BottomSheet standard/full/no-title), and `states.card.html` (all four EmptyState variants, both ErrorState variants, every Skeleton shape, PullToRefresh's three phases). Every card's viewport height was **measured from rendered content**, not estimated — the earlier round of clipped cards came from guessed heights.

### Intentional additions
The brand spec defines no formal component inventory (guidelines-only source), so a standard primitive set was authored, tuned to the brand. Four are brand-specific rather than generic — justified because they are the product's core mechanic ("words before faces"):
- **`PromptCard`** — a question answered in words; profiles lead with these.
- **`MessageBubble`** / **`VoiceNote`** — the conversation surface; encode the warm(you)/cool(them) rule.
- **`TypingDots`** — the three-dots logo device, reused as a brand motif.
- **`Avatar.blur`** — the signature "earn the face by having the conversation" reveal.

## RTL & Persian rules (قواعد RTL و فارسی)

This system is **Persian-first / RTL-first**: Persian is the source language (not a translation) and `direction: rtl` is the default. LTR is the mirrored/adapted case. For developers:

**Language & content**
- All **user-visible** text is native Persian — warm, sincere, a little poetic; it asks rather than announces (e.g. empty state: «هنوز گفتگویی شروع نشده — دربارهٔ چه چیزی می‌تونی ساعت‌ها حرف بزنی؟»). No slang, no hype, no emoji.
- **Code stays English.** Token names, component names, CSS classes, props, and technical docs remain English (`--conversation-active`, `Button`, `spacing-md`). Only rendered content is Persian — the codebase is not bilingual.
- نیم‌فاصله (ZWNJ) is used in Persian copy: «می‌شود»، «گفت‌وگو»، «هم‌صحبت».
- Persian punctuation: «،» and «؟». Bidi-sensitive runs (Latin words, the "Deltalk" logotype, hex codes) are isolated (`unicode-bidi: isolate`, `.dt-latin`, `dir="ltr"`) so punctuation doesn't jump.

**Direction & layout**
- `html { direction: rtl }` is set in `tokens/base.css`. Every card and the app kit declare `lang="fa" dir="rtl"`.
- Use **logical properties** — `margin-inline-start/end`, `padding-inline`, `inset-inline-*`, `text-align: start`, `border-inline` — never physical `left`/`right`. Component CSS was audited for this (Switch thumb, Avatar online dot, BottomNav badge, VoiceNote padding, MessageBubble meta all use logical props / `[dir="rtl"]` mirrors).
- **Isolate every Latin run inside an RTL block.** English prose in an RTL container reorders its trailing full stop to the line start, rendering as a stray leading period (`.variants ignore it`). Any English paragraph, caption or label carries `direction:ltr;text-align:left;unicode-bidi:isolate` on its own block — set on the CONTAINER, not per inline fragment (isolating each `<span>` individually leaves the runs ordered RTL relative to each other and scrambles the sentence order).
- **Directional icons** (back, send, chevrons, progress) mirror in RTL via `[dir="rtl"] [data-dir-icon]{transform:scaleX(-1)}` — tag them with `data-dir-icon`. **Non-directional icons** (heart, camera, settings, profile, compass) must NOT be tagged. In the app kit, `Icons.Back` and `Icons.Send` are directional; the rest are not.
- Speech-bubble tails mirror automatically (`[dir="rtl"] .dt-bubble--*`). Enter animations flow right-to-left.
- The Latin **Deltalk** logotype is the one exception and stays LTR (it ships as an SVG mark).

**Numbers & dates**
- UI numbers render as Persian digits (۰۱۲۳۴۵۶۷۸۹) — see the `toFa()` helper in the kit's `Data.jsx`. Timestamps and relative dates are Persian («۹:۴۱»، «دیروز»، «سه‌شنبه»); production dates should use the Jalali (هجری شمسی) calendar.

**Typography for Persian**
- Zero letter-spacing (all `--ls-*` tokens are `0`); generous line-heights (`--lh-normal: 1.75`, `--lh-relaxed: 1.9`); weight ≥ 400 at small sizes. **Titles and UI/body both render in IRANSans Mobile — the only family in the system.** Morabba and Vazirmatn were both removed: Morabba was self-hosted but unreachable through `--font-display`, and its opt-in `--font-display-serif` token had zero usages; Vazirmatn had no font file at all and was a dead fallback name.

**Unchanged from the base system:** the warm↔cool semantic color meaning, the one-hero-gradient rule, warm-paper surfaces, and all spacing / radii / heights / component structure. Only language and direction changed.

## Caveats / substitutions (please confirm)

### Superseded component values — do not reinstate

- **`--shadow-md` on `IconButton variant="solid"` → removed.** It was the variant's default elevation, on the assumption that a solid icon button floats. The census said otherwise: of 31 mounts, the only non-specimen consumers were the two composer sends, and both explicitly overrode it down to `--shadow-sm`. A default that every real consumer fights is a wrong default, so elevation moved to the floating consumer. Do not add it back as "missing".
- **`.dt-voice*` as a shared class prefix → split.** `messaging/VoicePlayer` and `conversation/VoiceNote` both used `.dt-voice*` **and** the same `useOnceStyle('dt-voice-css')` key. Since `useOnceStyle` bails when the id already exists, the second component to mount had its stylesheet silently dropped and rendered with the other's rules. Now `dt-vplayer-*` / `dt-vplayer-css` and `dt-vnote-*` / `dt-vnote-css`. **Every component's style id and class prefix must be unique to that component.**

- **`Card variant="accent"` → retired.** It was an `--accent-tint` surface added for the **Deltalk Plus** card, so a paid affordance could sit on a pink-tinted panel. Once that card adopted the neutral **Likes-tab upsell pattern**, the variant had **no consumer left** — and a tinted surface with no rule for *when* to reach for it gets picked for how it looks rather than what it means. `Card` is back to **raised | flat | cool | gradient**. (Same reasoning as the retired `--super-like` / `--boost` aliases: a value with zero consumers and no rule is a value that gets misused.)

Three occurrences before it was caught. **If a card's prose is being written through a script, the literal character goes in the source string — never the escape.**

**A second instance, 2026-08-02, in `VerificationBadge`:** the rosette's bounds were read off the path data as "3.85–20.15" — that is the `M` command's x, not the shape's extent, and the arcs bulge 1.1 units past it on every side. The wrong figure produced a viewBox that cropped 1.12 units off all four sides, slicing the axis-aligned lobes flat so the mark rendered as a **rounded square** — the exact failure the construction exists to prevent. **A path's `M` coordinate is not its bounding box.**

**Verify by rendered measurement, never by arithmetic.** `getBBox()` reports a group's *untransformed* user space and silently ignores the transform where all the lockup geometry lives — it will happily confirm a broken lockup. Measure client rects. ## Specimen rule — a specimen shows each variant in its HARDEST case, not its typical one

**The rule.** For every variant a card demonstrates, name the ground or the state where that variant has the **least separation** from what is behind it, and confirm that is what the specimen shows. A variant demonstrated only where it passes is not evidence that it passes.

**The test is concrete, and it is per variant, not per card.** Take each variant in turn and ask: what is the worst ground this can legitimately be mounted on, and what is the worst state it can be in? Then check that the card shows *that*. A fill-matching control (`secondary`, `outline`) is hardest on the surface its own fill copies. A borderless control (`ghost`) is hardest wherever it has neither fill nor border to fall back on. A control over media is hardest at the grey nearest its own luminance, which is found by sweeping, not by choosing a photograph. A disabled control is hardest in its lowest-weight variant, not its solid one. A focus indicator is hardest where the ring composites onto a ground of similar luminance.

**Three instances found so far, which is why this is written down.** Three of one shape is a habit rather than three accidents:

| # | found | the concealment | what it hid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-08-07 | `onMedia` shown on a dark strip only | the construction collapsed to **1.00:1** over bright photography; the defect shipped |
| 2 | 2026-08-07 | `DISABLED` shown as a solid fill only | disabled `ghost` — no fill, no border, dimmed label — was never demonstrated |
| 3 | 2026-08-07 | focus shown **nowhere at all** | the focus ring failed 3:1 on every surface in the system; found only when the specimen was built, fixed 2026-08-08 by going two-tone (see below) |

**The third is the instructive one.** The first two showed a variant in a flattering case; the third showed it in no case, and the absence hid a larger failure than either. **A missing specimen is the strongest form of this defect, not an exemption from it** — nothing is easier to overlook than a state that is not on the card, because there is no wrong-looking thing to notice. When auditing a card, list the states the component *has* before looking at what the card *shows*.

It was dropped for that reason and not for a stylistic one. Anything that cannot be shown to move a figure is **width that looks like a decision**: it reads as intentional to the next author, who then preserves it, and it costs real space in a construction that sits outside the control where space is contested. Brand identity is carried where a control can actually express it — a bordered control recolours its own border to `--focus-ring` — not by padding a contrast construction with a band that does no work.

### History — the two defects fixed first, and the one that could not be fixed by value

**Defect one — FIXED — focus REPLACED a variant's boundary instead of adding to it.** `.dt-iconbtn--onMedia` set `box-shadow: var(--on-media-halo)`; `.dt-iconbtn:focus-visible` set `box-shadow: var(--ring)`. A pseudo-class selector (0,2,0) outranks the variant class (0,1,0) and `box-shadow` does not accumulate across rules, so **the halo was dropped at the moment of focus** — the control was *least* separated from its ground exactly when focused. **The fix: every variant publishes its shadow as `--_shadow`, and the focus rule composes.** `.dt-btn` and `.dt-iconbtn` declare `--_shadow:0 0 #0000` — a transparent no-op, because the keyword `none` is illegal inside a box-shadow *list* and an unset `var()` would invalidate the whole declaration and drop the ring entirely. **A variant that adds a box-shadow must set `--_shadow`, never `box-shadow`.** `IconButton --outline` had lost its 1dp contour on focus for the same reason — one compositing bug, two variants.

**Defect two — FIXED — the dark ring was frozen light.** `--ring` was declared once in `:root` and composed `var(--focus-ring)`, so it resolved light and the computed value inherited into dark: every focused control in dark rendered `#F24C9A` while `--focus-ring` correctly read `#E481AF`. **The dark theme had never rendered its own focus colour.** Fixed by declaring all three sites; `--glow-warm` and `--glow-cool` carried the identical defect and were fixed with it. Moving to a theme-independent two-tone ring later made `--ring`'s own case moot, but the glows still depend on the rule.

**The bookkeeping around defect two is worth keeping**, because three sets of dark numbers existed. ## On-media plate — derivation (rulings 26a–26e, 2026-08-07)

The shipped construction is `--on-media-plate` (`--bg` at 70%) + `--on-media-halo`, glyph `--on-media-ink`, theme-following. Measured: glyph and text 8.01:1 light (worst at `#000000`) / 6.23:1 dark (worst at `#FFFFFF`); boundary 3.89:1 light at `#FAFAFA` / 5.85:1 dark at `#191919`. Hover lifts to `--surface-card` at the same alpha: 8.35:1 light, 5.53:1 dark. Everything below is why, kept so nobody re-derives it.

**The alpha.** Each theme is the other's mirror, so the worst case sits at the opposite end of the ramp in each and neither can be extrapolated from the other; both were swept independently over all 256 greys. Floors, per bar:

| bar | light | dark |
|---|---|---|
| glyph 3:1 | α 0.41 | α 0.48 |
| text 4.5:1 | α 0.52 | α 0.61 |
| boundary 3:1 | α 0.57 | α 0.62 |

The lowest single alpha clearing every bar in both themes is 0.62, and it lands on exactly 3.00:1 in dark (ground `#929292`) — no margin. **0.70 ships** for headroom. `AgeChip` and `DistanceChip` carry text and therefore set the 4.5:1 bar; the `IconButton` glyph would have cleared lower and does not govern.

**The rejected .20-white plate.** An earlier translucent construction kept a WHITE glyph on a white-ish plate and collapsed to 1.00:1 over anything brighter than ~48% grey — sky, snow, sand, a white wall. Inverting the glyph per theme is what makes translucency legal here; it reverses the failure direction rather than removing it.

**Blur is not a boundary.** `backdrop-filter: blur()` changes no luminance and contributes nothing to contrast at any alpha. No figure anywhere credits it.

**The ring, and why it is gone.** While one 1dp stroke both set the control's visual weight and held the boundary, nothing lighter than `--text-primary` was legal: `--border-control` bottomed at 1.60:1 light / 1.73:1 dark, `--border-strong` 1.32 / 1.55, `--border` 1.19 / 1.34. | ring | light | dark |
|---|---|---|
| none | 3.89:1 @ `#FAFAFA` | 5.85:1 @ `#191919` |
| `--border` | 3.89:1 | 5.85:1 |
| `--border-control` | 3.89:1 | 5.85:1 |

Identical, because at every ground where the plate fails the halo already dominates. **A lever is only free once something else carries its load** — the same shape as the plate-and-ring pairing rule.

**Failed shadow levers.** `--shadow-on-photo` is a BLACK blur: it helps in light theme by darkening a bright photo away from a light plate, and hurts in dark by pulling the photo toward a dark plate. Black-only halos fall to 1.80–2.07:1 in dark at every useful alpha, so the halo had to be theme-following. It is a HARD 1dp contour rather than a blur as a measurement decision: a blur's alpha at the boundary is roughly half nominal, and `0 1px 4px` at .90 measures 3.33:1 in light on that model but 2.77:1 on a 0.4× one. Light's worst ground `#FAFAFA` is where the plate vanishes and the halo is the only separator — no slack for a modelling assumption.

**The halo is a visible line, deliberately.** Light theme over bright photography it reads 3.81:1 against the plate: a discernible dark hairline, exactly where it is the only thing separating the control from the photo. Over dark photography it recedes into the ground (8.80:1). Dark theme is milder both ways (5.41:1 / 6.36:1).

## Hazard — theme-following aliases must be declared per theme, never once in `:root`

A custom property whose value is `var(--bg)` resolves **where it is declared**, not where it is used. An alias declared once in `:root` therefore freezes to the `:root` (light) values, and `[data-theme="dark"]` inherits it already-resolved. A descendant theme selector cannot reach back into it.

This shipped undetected: the `--on-media-*` set was declared only in `:root`, so in the dark panel `--text-primary` was `#F8FAFC` while the glyph computed `rgb(18,25,38)` on a light plate. **Dark theme rendered the light construction from ruling 26b until 26e**, and every dark figure recorded across those three rulings was measured against the wrong render. Nothing in the console flags it; the tokens all resolve, just to the wrong values.

**The correct pattern:** any alias of a theme-dependent token is redeclared in each theme block, alongside the tokens it aliases. Keep `:root`, `[data-theme="light"]` and `[data-theme="dark"]` in sync — the "mirrors `:root`, keep in sync" rule the theme blocks already carry applies to aliases too, and an alias cannot opt out of it.

## Buttons — derivation

**Why rank is weight, not hue.** Solid → bordered → transparent survives the theme flip and colour-blind viewers; a hue-ranked hierarchy does not. The `gradient` variant is the one sanctioned exception and exists for brand moments (start a conversation, first match), at most one per screen.

**Why there is no cool/accent button variant.** Cool means "the other person / conversation active" — a relationship, not a button rank. A strong colour carrying no rank gets picked for how it looks rather than what it means. Cool keeps that meaning where it belongs: `Avatar ring="cool"`, `Badge variant="active"`, the typing indicator, read receipts.

**Shape is meaning, not taste.** Rounded square is the default — a chrome utility whose label didn't fit, and it must still read as a button. `shape="circle"` is an explicit opt-in for controls floating over content or in the avatar family.

**Icons.** Text alone is the default; an icon is added only when it names the action faster than the word does.

**The prop names are the misleading part.** `iconLeft`/`iconRight` render in DOM order, so in RTL `iconLeft` lands at the visual right. The mirroring is correct; the names are legacy.

**The touch minimum is the component's job, not the consumer's.** Button sm (40) and IconButton 36/40 keep their visual size and gain a transparent inset to `--size-48` inside the component, so no call site has to leave clear space around them. The label and the glyph never grow to reach the minimum — growing the mark to hit a hit-area figure changes the design to satisfy a measurement.

**The grounds on the specimen are the hardest cases.** This card once demonstrated `onMedia` on a dark strip only, which is how the 1.00:1 bright-ground failure shipped. Each strip now contains the measured worst ground for one theme — a `#191919` band on the photo strip, `#FAFAFA` on the bright one — so a passing specimen means the worst case passed.

## Overlays — derivation

**Dialog vs ConfirmDialog.** Dialog is the general bottom sheet for a decision with room for prose and any number of actions. ConfirmDialog is the fixed two-action confirmation, centred, with an icon halo that turns danger-tinted for `variant="destructive"`. Its confirm button is the only place the danger fill belongs on a button.

**Why the sort sheet uses Radio, not settings rows.** A chevron promises a deeper screen. Single-select inside a sheet is `Radio`, the system's single-select control.

**One media slot, two forms.** `icon` is shorthand that resolves into `media`, so halo consumers are unchanged. The slot owns position and ground; the node owns its fill — pass the bare glyph. Avatar in the slot is xl / 112dp.

**Why the media node needs a backing.** The first consumer is a one-time "you liked Sara" modal, and the badge is a heart — the like that just happened, not a verification mark. The bare gradient heart fails on both light grounds: 2.35:1 on light warm portrait `#E8D5C4` and 2.88:1 on cream `#F4EDE3` at the pink stop, 2.74:1 on warm at the purple stop. The ink backing carries it instead — 12.36:1 / 15.15:1 on the light grounds, with a 2dp white ring taking over in dark (12.32:1 / 17.79:1), and the heart reads 5.16:1 on the backing. The measured table stays on the card because those are the figures a consumer checks; this paragraph is why the backing exists.

**Stage geometry on the specimen card (measured, not guessed).** The stages were 240px, which squeezed ConfirmDialog — max-width 380 + 24px scrim padding per side = 428 to render untruncated — to about half its designed measure, so every h2 title wrapped and a true-size 112dp avatar looked oversized beside a halo that was not scaled at all. Both were the same defect; widening to 428 fixed them together. Stage height is 520: the tallest panel is the destructive ConfirmDialog at 452px, plus 24px of scrim padding top and bottom. The media block uses its own 2-track grid because the 3-up grid wrapped it 3+1 and orphaned the dark halo panel a row below its pair.

**Card CSS notes.** The stage's `transform` is load-bearing — it creates a containing block so Dialog/ConfirmDialog's `position:fixed` scrim is trapped inside the stage instead of covering the card; BottomSheet's scrim is already absolute and needs only the relative parent. The English note sets `direction` on the container: isolating each mono fragment individually leaves the runs ordered RTL relative to each other and scrambles the sentence.
