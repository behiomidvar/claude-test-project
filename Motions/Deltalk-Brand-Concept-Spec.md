# Deltalk — Brand Concept Spec

> گفتگویی که از دل برمی‌آید — *the conversation that comes from the heart.*

This is the foundation document for the Deltalk brand. Everything downstream — the pitch deck, the brand guidelines, and the Android app — should trace back to what's written here. If a design or copy decision can't be justified against this spec, it's off-brand.

---

## 1. The idea in one line

**Deltalk is where you talk your way to a connection — instead of swiping your way past one.**

Most connection apps ask you to judge a face in half a second. Deltalk asks you to say something real first. The heart in the logo is a speech bubble for a reason: on Deltalk, the conversation *is* the attraction.

---

## 2. What Deltalk is

Deltalk is a conversation-first connection app. People come to find a partner, a friend, or simply a هم‌صحبت — someone worth talking to. The entry point is always words: a voice note, an answer to a prompt, a real message. Faces come second, not first.

It is deliberately broader than "dating." The same loneliness that makes romance hard in adulthood also makes friendship and everyday companionship hard. Deltalk serves the whole spectrum — **friendship → companionship → romance** — because for its core users these needs overlap, and forcing people to declare "I'm only here for X" is part of what makes the other apps feel cold.

**The signature mechanic:** you meet through conversation, and the connection deepens as you talk. Photos can start soft and clarify as a real exchange builds; profiles lead with a prompt or a voice note, not a grid of selfies. Whatever the exact UI, the principle is fixed — *earn the face by having the conversation.* This is the one thing no competitor owns, and it comes straight out of the name and the logo.

---

## 3. The insight — why Deltalk should exist

Finding real connection in adult life has quietly gotten harder. People move cities, work remotely, leave the friend-making machinery of school and university behind. The apps that are supposed to solve this were built for fast, visual, high-volume judgement — a format that rewards the most photogenic 24-year-old and exhausts everyone else.

The people who feel this most acutely aren't only the young. They're also men and women in their late 30s, 40s, and early 50s — divorced, relocated, widowed, or simply grown past the scene — for whom the existing apps feel loud, shallow, and slightly humiliating. They don't want a game. They want a conversation.

Deltalk is built for the moment someone thinks: *I'm good at talking to people once I actually get to talk to them. I just need somewhere that lets me.*

---

## 4. Who Deltalk is for

Deltalk has two audiences. They look different but share one truth: **they're better in conversation than they are in a photo grid.**

### Primary — the strategic core: adults 35–50
Men and women, roughly 35 to 50, for whom meeting people in real life has become difficult and who turn to apps out of genuine need, not entertainment. Divorced or single-again, relocated, focused on work or raising kids, tired of nightlife as a venue for meeting people. They're seeking a partner, a friend, or a real هم‌صحبت — often more than one of those at once.

- **What they feel:** existing apps are built for someone else — younger, more casual, more performative. Swiping feels like being judged and judging others on the thinnest possible evidence.
- **What they want:** slowness, warmth, substance, and the dignity of being known for how they think, not just how they photograph.
- **Why they're the core:** they are underserved, they have real intent, and they convert to paying users because the need is real. Winning them is Deltalk's wedge.

### Secondary — younger users (roughly 20–34)
Young people who are burned out on swipe culture and want something that feels more human and less like a slot machine. They keep the app energetic and social, and they're comfortable with voice notes and prompts.

- **What they feel:** swipe fatigue, "everyone's the same," conversations that never start or die instantly.
- **What they want:** a lower-pressure, more genuine way in — and a brand that doesn't feel like a nightclub.

### The shared user, in one sentence
*"I connect through conversation, and I want an app that lets me lead with that."*

> **Design consequence:** the brand must feel welcoming and grown-up enough for a 47-year-old, yet warm and alive enough for a 26-year-old. The way to hold both is warmth + sincerity, never youth-slang or nightlife energy. Age-neutral warmth is the target. When a decision would skew young (neon, slang, party imagery), pull it back toward warm and human.

---

## 5. Mission, vision, values

**Mission.** To help people connect through real conversation — and to make that the easiest, warmest way to meet someone, whatever kind of connection they're looking for.

**Vision.** A world where "we just talked and it clicked" is how people meet again — where the first thing you learn about someone is what they have to say.

**Values (the five we design and write by):**

1. **Words before faces.** Conversation is the product. Every feature should make the first real exchange easier, warmer, sooner.
2. **Warm, not hot.** Deltalk is about affection and curiosity, never heat or hookup energy. If it feels like a nightclub, it's wrong.
3. **Slow is a feature.** We reward patience and depth over volume and speed. We never optimize for endless swiping.
4. **Dignity by default.** Every user — especially the older, the shy, the starting-over — should feel respected, safe, and seen.
5. **Real over perfect.** We celebrate genuine, slightly imperfect humans over polished performances.

## 6. Positioning

Every competitor owns a mechanic. Deltalk owns **the conversation.**

| App | Owns | Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| Tinder | The swipe | Fast, visual, casual |
| Bumble | Women message first | Empowered, still photo-led |
| Hinge | "Designed to be deleted" | Intentional, prompt-aware |
| Mamba / Badoo | Scale and reach | Broad, high-volume |
| **Deltalk** | **Talk first — the conversation is the attraction** | **Warm, sincere, unhurried** |

**Positioning statement.**
*For adults who find real-life connection hard and are tired of being judged on a photo, Deltalk is the connection app where you meet through conversation instead of swiping — because the way someone talks tells you more than the way they look.*

**One-line version for the deck:** *Talk first. دل به دل راه داره.*

**What we deliberately avoid:** fire/flame/heat metaphors (Tinder's territory), swipe-stack mechanics as the hero, hookup-coded imagery, and anything that treats people as a deck of cards. Our differentiation is emotional and mechanical at once — we can't out-swipe the incumbents, so we don't try.

## 7. The logo, decoded

The mark is the strategy. Read it out loud and the whole brand falls out of it.

- **It's a heart made of a speech bubble.** One shape, two meanings: love and conversation are the same gesture on Deltalk. This is the single most important visual truth in the brand.
- **It's two temperatures meeting.** The warm arm (apricot → coral → magenta) is the heart reaching out — دل. The cool arm (indigo → violet → lavender) is the words coming back — talk. Two different people, two different temperatures, fusing into one shape. That seam where warm meets cool *is* دل به دل راه داره: the path between two hearts.
- **The three dots are a typing indicator.** Someone is about to speak. The brand's core emotion lives here — anticipation, the warmth of "they're replying." Use the three dots as a recurring brand device: loading states, section breaks, the deck's build animations, the app's "someone's typing" moments.

**How to use the duality:** treat warm and cool as *two voices in one conversation.* Warm = the user, the reach, the feeling, the primary action. Cool = the other person, the reply, the calm structure. Never blend them into mush — let them meet at a clean seam, the way they do in the logo.

## 8. Personality & voice

**If Deltalk were a person:** the friend who's genuinely good at introducing people — warm, curious, a little poetic, emotionally intelligent, never crude. Old enough to be sincere, young enough to be playful. Speaks to a 26-year-old and a 48-year-old the same way, because warmth doesn't have an age.

**Voice principles:**

- **Ask, don't announce.** Prefer questions to statements. Empty states ask something instead of apologizing: *"No matches yet — what could you talk about all night?"*
- **Short, warm sentences.** Second person. Plain words with occasional poetry.
- **Sincere over clever.** A little heart-forward earnestness beats a witty one-liner. We can be warm without being saccharine.
- **Never horny, never hype.** No heat language, no growth-hack urgency, no "HURRY, 3 people liked you!!" No slang that dates the brand or excludes the older user.
- **Persian-first, bilingual by design.** Persian is the primary language of the brand; English is the adaptation. Copy is written natively in Persian first — warm and literary — and never reads like localized English. The tagline lives in both, but Persian leads.

**Voice, quick contrast:**

- ✅ "Say something real. See who says something back."
- ❌ "Swipe now — hot singles near you 🔥"
- ✅ "You two have been talking for a while. Want to say hello properly?"
- ❌ "It's a match! 🎉🔥"

## 9. Color

Pulled directly from the logo file. The palette is a **warm-to-cool system**, not a set of loose swatches — the relationship between the two families is the brand.

**Warm family — the heart (دل).** Used for the user, primary actions, affection, energy.
- Apricot `#FEAC6A`
- Coral `#F77585`
- Magenta `#EB4CA6`

**Cool family — the words (talk).** Used for the other person, structure, calm, "conversation active."
- Indigo `#261D82`
- Violet `#5952E4`
- Lavender `#896EF4`

**The Deltalk gradient (signature).** Magenta `#EB4CA6` → Indigo `#261D82` — the seam where warm meets cool. This is the hero gradient: reserve it for the logo, key brand moments, and one hero element per screen or slide. Overusing it kills it.

**Neutrals.** A warm off-white surface rather than clinical white keeps the brand human (e.g. a soft paper tone around `#FBF3E9`), with a near-black ink for text (`#1A1626`) so the warmth carries even in the type.

**Discipline rules (carry into guidelines and app):**
- One hero gradient moment per screen. Everything else is flat warm or flat cool.
- Warm and cool each carry *meaning* — don't scatter them decoratively. If cool means "the other person / conversation active," keep it meaning that everywhere.
- Check contrast: magenta and coral text on light backgrounds need care; prefer the darker end of each family for text on tint.

## 11. What Deltalk is NOT

A short list that protects the brand. Put a version of this in the guidelines as "what not to do."

- **Not a hookup app.** No heat, no flame, no nightclub. Warm ≠ hot.
- **Not a swipe game.** The swipe stack is not our hero mechanic and shouldn't be treated as one.
- **Not for the young only.** Never let imagery, slang, or energy exclude the 40-something core user.
- **Not performative.** We don't reward the most polished profile; we reward the best conversation.
- **Not cold or clinical.** No pure white, no corporate blue, no sterile minimalism. Human warmth over tech gloss.

## 12. How this carries across the three deliverables

Same brand, three expressions of the warm/cool duality.

**Pitch deck.** Dark indigo (`#261D82`) backgrounds; warm apricot/coral for the one number that matters per slide; the Deltalk gradient reserved for the title and the "differentiator" slide. Every section opener is a *question* in the display serif — mirroring the app's ask-don't-announce voice. Use the three-dots device as the build/transition motif. Lead the story with the 35–50 insight; it's the most investable part of the concept.

**Brand guidelines.** The inverse surface — warm paper background, generous margins. Suggested structure: name story → the insight & audience → positioning → mission & values → logo (construction, clear space, the warm/cool rule, the three-dots device) → color (with contrast ratios and the "one hero gradient" rule) → type scale (with an explicit Persian / RTL section) → voice with do/don't examples → motion (the unfold, not the swipe) → UI components → what-not-to-do → **a stated safety principle** (see below).

**Android app.** Material 3 with dynamic color disabled. Warm paper as the surface instead of pure white. Warm family = the user and primary actions; cool family reserved for "conversation active" and the other person. One hero gradient moment per screen, never more. **The app is RTL-first. Persian is the primary market, so the app is designed in RTL from the ground up — layouts, navigation, iconography, and copy are Persian-native first, with LTR/English as the adaptation.** Retrofitting RTL onto an LTR layout later would be the single most expensive mistake in this project, so it's decided up front. Motion signature: nothing swipes away; things *unfold* — the reveal is the gesture, echoing "the connection deepens as you talk."

---

## 13. Safety as brand, not just settings

A conversation-first connection app serving vulnerable, starting-over adults carries a real duty of care. Treat safety as a brand value, not a buried menu:

- Reporting, blocking, and (if the reveal mechanic ships) explicit photo-consent should be first-class, visible, and warmly worded.
- State the principle in the guidelines: *dignity and safety by default.* It strengthens the "warm and human" positioning rather than diluting it — and investors will ask.
